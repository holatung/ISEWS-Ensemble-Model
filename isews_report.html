ISEWS Shutdown Monitor

The Internet Shutdown Early Warning System (ISEWS) scores the risk of full or regional shutdowns, social media and app bans, IP blocking, DNS/HTTP censorship, VPN targeting, bandwidth throttling, protocol downgrades, targeted slowdowns and surveillance. It fuses three inputs: a structural risk model trained on country-month history, a trigger feed, and deep-learning detectors that read 14 network-telemetry channels.

03 · Interference taxonomy

Nine modalities, each with a telemetry signature

The severity weight scales a modality's probability into detection strength D. Signatures come from the documented cases. The Iran stealth-blackout signature, for example, follows Project Ainita (IETF 123) and Aryapour (2025): BGP stays up, traffic falls about 97%, only ports 53, 80 and 443 pass, and DNS answers point to 10.10.34.34.

04 · Models

Machine learning and deep learning components

Five model families run in three layers. All metrics are from held-out simulated data. They show the pipeline works end to end and are not estimates of real-world accuracy.

Machine learningLayer 1 · structural risk

Country-month shutdown risk

The models train on 4,200 country-months (2016-2022) and are tested on 1,800 (2023-2025), a temporal split across 50 countries. The positive rate is 15.6%. Each country's latent propensity is anchored to its 2025 Access Now count. S, the structural risk, is the equal-weight mean of the three models.

Deep learningLayer 2 · modality detection

Dilated temporal CNN, multi-label

input 24 h × 14 channels (z-scored) Conv1D 14→32 k=3 d=1 ReLU Conv1D 32→32 k=3 d=2 ReLU Conv1D 32→32 k=3 d=4 ReLU receptive field 15 h pool [mean ‖ max] over time → 64 Dense 64→9 sigmoid (one per modality) loss class-weighted BCE · Adam · cosine LR params 8,169 · NumPy, hand-derived gradients

The network trains on 9,000 windows and is tested on 3,000. Macro F1 is 0.952 and macro AUC 0.999. The ML baseline, gradient boosting on 84 engineered window features, scores macro F1 0.986. On clean simulated signatures the hand-built features do slightly better. The TCN learns from raw sequences instead, so it needs no re-engineering when censors change tactics.

Both detectors flag benign outages such as power cuts and cable faults, which is expected because an anomaly detector cannot see intent. In those cases the modality classifier and human review make the call. Operators blamed undersea cables during Kenya's June 2024 disruption, which is exactly this ambiguity.

Layer 3 · fusion and alerting

ISEWS score

score = 100 × [1 − (1 − 0.35·S)(1 − 0.55·E)(1 − 0.80·D)] S structural 30-day risk (ensemble mean) E trigger escalation from the ACLED/news feed (0-1) D max( maxₖ pₖ·severityₖ , 0.6·A )

The noisy-OR form lets any one strong signal raise the score, and no single input can push it past its own ceiling. Context alone (S and E) can reach Warning, so alerts can go out before telemetry moves. Every alert at Warning or above goes to a human reviewer before tiered release by API and WhatsApp.

Roadmap models (not yet built)

  • LSTM or Temporal Fusion Transformer forecaster for 72-hour risk curves from the escalation feed.
  • Graph neural network over the AS-level topology, to locate where a cut is made (gateway, IXP or operator).
  • Multilingual transformer (for example AfroXLMR) to pull trigger events from news and social posts in Hausa, Yoruba, Swahili, Amharic and Tigrinya.
  • Calibration with isotonic regression on the real STOP labels, and conformal intervals on the score.

Conflicting figures

  • CPJ (Feb 2026) states Russia recorded the highest number of shutdowns worldwide in 2025; Access Now ranks Myanmar first (95) and Russia fourth (19). The two organisations count differently, so ISEWS keeps Access Now as the single source for its training labels.
  • Access Now's 2025 press release gives 304 shutdowns for 2024. Earlier 2024 reporting gave a lower figure, so the 2024 total appears to have been revised. ISEWS uses the revised figure.
  • Amnezia is a VPN vendor. Its Iran figures (87-day shutdown, Russian technical assistance) are used for context only and are not training labels.

08 · Limitations and sources

What this prototype does not prove

  • All telemetry, the training panel and every score are simulated. The high detection scores reflect clean, designed signatures. Real traffic is noisier and censors adapt.
  • Regime and conflict codes for the 50 panel countries are approximate analyst codes. Replace them with V-Dem and ACLED data.
  • Escalation curves are hand-set stand-ins for an ACLED and news feed. Tigray escalation follows the ChatVE brief's sequence for 23-24 September 2026.
  • The fusion weights (0.35, 0.55, 0.80) and the severity weights are design choices, not fitted values. Tune them on backtests before deployment.
  • National aggregates hide regional cuts, as the Ethiopia national-view card shows. Deployment needs per-ASN and subnational feeds.
  • A wrong alert in a conflict zone has real costs, so release stays gated by human review.
